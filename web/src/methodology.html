PROJECT MERCURY TEAM / METHODS & SOURCES

Wonder, with
the evidence attached.

SKYSHIFT is an independent Project Mercury Team application for inspecting calibrated SPHEREx DR1 and QR3 observations. Each exposure retains its archive source, measurement date, detector and wavelength information.

FROM CALIBRATED EXPOSURE TO SKY VIEW

A common sky grid.

Observations opens dated cutouts from bundled challenge previews or searches IRSA by sky position. The four viewing modes in Compare use a north-up ICRS tangent grid with an angular sampling of 6.15″ per pixel.

When grids differ, the viewer maps exposures to the same grid using the world coordinate system and the available valid pixels. The target marker accounts for the preset’s proper motion at the observation epoch.

The asinh stretch, optional median background subtraction and display-only gap fill affect the view. “Show masked pixels” exposes missing or flagged samples. Difference and coadds use usable measurements.

Check the dates, wavelengths, release, masks and source records before interpreting a difference. The tools support visual inspection. Quantitative motion and variability require separate calibration and analysis.

DATA SOURCES & CREDITS

Where the data comes from.

SPHEREx DR1 and QR3

The dated cutouts use calibrated Level 2 spectral images from NASA/IPAC IRSA. DR1 reprocesses Surveys 1 and 2. QR3 covers Surveys 3 and 4, with additional data released as it becomes available. SPHEREx is a NASA-funded project of JPL and Caltech. This work uses the NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive, operated by Caltech under contract with NASA. Cite Bock et al. (2026), ApJ 999, 139, and Akeson et al. (2026), ApJS 285, 67.

SPHEREx mission archive ↗ · Release documentation ↗ · DR1 DOI ↗ · QR3 DOI ↗

SIMBAD target astrometry

Challenge coordinates and proper motions use SIMBAD, operated by CDS in Strasbourg. Cite Wenger et al. (2000), A&AS 143, 9. SKYSHIFT propagates the reference position to each observation epoch. The five preset records include their original astrometric citations.

SIMBAD at CDS ↗ · Target source records ↗

Gaia DR3 and AllWISE overlays

The optional Gaia overlay uses ESA Gaia Data Release 3, processed by the Gaia Data Processing and Analysis Consortium (DPAC) with funding from national institutions, in particular the institutions participating in the Gaia Multilateral Agreement, and served through MAST at STScI. Cite Gaia Collaboration, Vallenari et al. (2023), A&A 674, A1. AllWISE combines NASA-funded WISE and NEOWISE survey data. SKYSHIFT queries the AllWISE source catalog, II/328 (Cutri et al., 2013), through CDS VizieR.

Gaia at MAST ↗ · AllWISE release and citation ↗ · CDS VizieR citation ↗

Aladin Lite and DSS Color

The optional optical background uses CDS Aladin Lite (Boch & Fernique, 2014, ASP Conference Series 485, 277). DSS photographic plates were digitized by STScI. CDS produced the color HiPS tiles served through the MAST mirror. DSS and its derived color tiles are available for scientific, educational and nonprofit use under the MAST-listed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 terms. Original plate copyright depends on the survey.

Aladin Lite and license ↗ · DSS and MAST data-use policy ↗ · Bundled source and licence ↗

CADC and WiseView

The Canadian Astronomy Data Centre link points to the NEOSSat archive from CSA and DRDC. SKYSHIFT has no matched NEOSSat observations. WiseView, developed for Backyard Worlds, inspired the blink interaction. SKYSHIFT uses SPHEREx pixels for its flipbook.

NEOSSat at CADC ↗ · WiseView project and citation ↗
